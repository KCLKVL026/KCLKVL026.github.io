Time Constant & Charging/Discharging Rules

⏱️ Time Constant \(\tau\) \(\tau = R \cdot C\) Unit: seconds (s)
📐 RC Circuit Charging/Discharging Formulas
Charging phase ( \(v_{\text{in}} = V_m\) ): \(v_C(t) = V_m + (V_0 - V_m)e^{-t/\tau}\)
Discharging phase ( \(v_{\text{in}} = 0\) ): \(v_C(t) = 0 + (V_0 - 0)e^{-t/\tau}\)
\(\tau = RC\) determines transient speed: after \(1\tau\) rises/drops ~63.2%, after \(5\tau\) essentially reaches steady state.
The ratio of square wave frequency to \(\tau\) affects the capacitor voltage waveform shape (linear charging/exponential charging and discharging).
💡 Key observations: When period \(T \ll \tau\), capacitor charging/discharging is incomplete, appearing as an approximate triangle wave; when \(T \gg \tau\), capacitor voltage can fully charge or discharge, waveform approaches a square wave.
Current default parameters: R=1kΩ, C=2μF → τ=0.002s, period T=0.02s, half-period 0.01s, charging/discharging takes one time constant, waveform shows prominent exponential characteristics.

🔍 Waveform Observation Points

• Square wave rising edge: capacitor begins charging, voltage rises exponentially toward \(V_m\).
• Square wave falling edge: capacitor discharges through resistor, voltage decays exponentially toward 0.
• Larger time constant \(\tau = RC\) means slower charging/discharging, more "sluggish" waveform.
• Adjust frequency: at low frequencies, capacitor can fully charge/discharge; at high frequencies, waveform shows linear triangle trend.

⚙️ Parameter Influence Rules

\(v_C(t)\) charging rate is determined by \(\tau = R \cdot C\).
Increasing \(R\) or \(C\) → \(\tau\) increases → charging/discharging slows down, waveform distortion becomes more pronounced;
Increasing square wave frequency \(f\) → period decreases → charging/discharging time shortens, capacitor voltage amplitude decreases.
Amplitude \(V_m\) only changes voltage amplitude, not the time constant.
