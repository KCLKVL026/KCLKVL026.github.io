Fourier Series · Trapezoidal Wave Decomposition and Harmonic Superposition

A trapezoidal wave can be decomposed into the superposition of odd sine harmonics, with spectral decay rate between that of the square wave and triangle wave.
Trapezoidal wave parameters: rising/falling edges each occupy 20% of the half-period (\(0.1\,\text{s}\)), flat top occupies 60% (\(0.3\,\text{s}\)), amplitude \(\pm1\).
The charts below show the fundamental wave, and the process of progressively adding 3rd, 5th, 7th, 11th, 33rd, and 55th harmonics to approximate a trapezoidal wave.

Fourier Series (Trapezoidal Wave)

Fourier series of a periodic trapezoidal wave (amplitude \(\pm1\), rise time \(\tau_r\), flat top time \(\tau_p\), fall time \(\tau_f\)) contains only odd sine terms:

\[ f(t) = \sum_{n=1,3,5,\ldots}^{\infty} b_n \sin(n\omega t) \]

Coefficients \(b_n\) decay as \(n^2\) (when rise/fall edges are non-zero), converging faster than the square wave. This demo uses numerical integration to precisely compute each harmonic coefficient.
Parameters: period \(T=1\,\text{s}\), rising edge length \(0.1\,\text{s}\), flat top length \(0.3\,\text{s}\), falling edge length \(0.1\,\text{s}\).

📌 Horizontal axis is time \(t\) (seconds), fundamental frequency \(f = 1\,\text{Hz}\), showing two complete periods. Trapezoidal wave edges are smooth, harmonic decay is fast, and finite-term synthesis already closely approximates the waveform.

Key Observations

  • The fundamental is a sine wave with amplitude ~0.93 (slightly larger than the triangle wave fundamental).
  • Adding the 3rd harmonic, the waveform begins to show a "trapezoidal" outline with small ripples in the flat top region.
  • Adding 5th and 7th harmonics reduces flat top ripples, and rising/falling edges steepen.
  • Adding 11th, 33rd, and 55th harmonics makes the waveform nearly coincide with the ideal trapezoidal wave, with negligible Gibbs overshoot.
  • Due to the presence of rising/falling edges, the trapezoidal wave's high-frequency components decay rapidly, and synthesis convergence is superior to the square wave.
