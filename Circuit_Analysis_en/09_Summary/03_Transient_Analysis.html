7.1 Transient Process and Switching Rules

7.1.1 Basic Concepts of Dynamic Circuits

  • Dynamic Circuit: A circuit containing energy-storage elements such as capacitors or inductors
  • Transient Process (Transition Process): The intermediate process when a circuit transitions from one steady state to another
  • Cause: Energy in energy-storage elements cannot change instantaneously (energy accumulation and release require time)
  • Steady State: The state where all quantities in the circuit have reached stability (DC steady state or AC steady state)
  • Switching: A sudden change in circuit structure or parameters (switch operation, parameter change, etc.)

7.1.2 Switching Rules

Core Theorem Switching Rules

Statement: At the switching instant ($t = 0$), capacitor voltage and inductor current cannot change instantaneously

$$u_C(0^+) = u_C(0^-) \qquad i_L(0^+) = i_L(0^-)$$

Physical Basis: Energy cannot change instantaneously ($W_C = \frac{1}{2}Cu_C^2$, $W_L = \frac{1}{2}Li_L^2$)

Corollaries:

  • Capacitor current can change instantaneously
  • Inductor voltage can change instantaneously
  • Resistor voltage and current can both change instantaneously

2

7.2 First-Order Circuit Zero-Input Response

RC Circuit First-Order RC Circuit Zero-Input Response

Circuit Structure: A charged capacitor C discharging through a resistor R

$$RC\frac{du_C}{dt} + u_C = 0$$ $$u_C(t) = u_C(0^+)e^{-t/\tau}$$
  • Time Constant: $\tau = RC$ (unit: seconds)
  • Capacitor voltage decays exponentially to zero
  • Larger $\tau$ means slower decay
  • After $3\tau \sim 5\tau$, the transient process can be considered essentially complete
$$i(t) = -\frac{u_C(0^+)}{R}e^{-t/\tau}$$

Discharge Current: Maximum at the start, zero at the end

RL Circuit First-Order RL Circuit Zero-Input Response

Circuit Structure: An energized inductor L releasing energy through a resistor R

$$L\frac{di_L}{dt} + Ri_L = 0$$ $$i_L(t) = i_L(0^+)e^{-t/\tau}$$
  • Time Constant: $\tau = L/R$ (unit: seconds)
  • Inductor current decays exponentially to zero
  • Inductor voltage $u_L(t) = -Ri_L(0^+)e^{-t/\tau}$ (changes instantaneously)

7.3 First-Order Circuit Zero-State Response

RC Circuit First-Order RC Circuit Zero-State Response

Circuit Structure: An uncharged capacitor C connected to a DC voltage source $U_s$

$$RC\frac{du_C}{dt} + u_C = U_s$$ $$u_C(t) = U_s(1 - e^{-t/\tau}), \quad \tau = RC$$
  • Capacitor voltage rises exponentially from zero to $U_s$
  • Charging current $i(t) = \frac{U_s}{R}e^{-t/\tau}$, maximum at start, zero at end
  • Capacitor acts as an open circuit (DC steady state)
RL Circuit First-Order RL Circuit Zero-State Response

Circuit Structure: An inductor L with zero current connected to a DC voltage source $U_s$ (in series with resistor R)

$$L\frac{di_L}{dt} + Ri_L = U_s$$ $$i_L(t) = \frac{U_s}{R}(1 - e^{-t/\tau}), \quad \tau = L/R$$
  • Inductor current rises exponentially from zero to $U_s/R$
  • Inductor voltage $u_L(t) = U_s e^{-t/\tau}$, maximum at start, zero at end
  • Inductor acts as a short circuit (DC steady state)

7.4 Full Response and Three-Element Method

7.4.1 Full Response of First-Order Circuits

Definition Full Response

Definition: The sum of the zero-input response and the zero-state response

$$\text{Full Response} = \text{Zero-Input Response} + \text{Zero-State Response}$$

Second Decomposition: Steady-state component + Transient component

$$\text{Full Response} = \text{Steady-State Component} + \text{Transient Component}$$

RC Circuit Full Response:

$$u_C(t) = U_s + [u_C(0^+) - U_s]e^{-t/\tau}$$
  • $U_s$: Steady-state component (final value)
  • $[u_C(0^+) - U_s]e^{-t/\tau}$: Transient component (decaying term)
  • Physical Meaning: The full response reflects the complete change process of the circuit from its initial state to steady state

7.4.2 Three-Element Method for First-Order Circuits

Key Method Three-Element Method

Three Elements:

  • Initial Value $f(0^+)$: The value immediately after switching
  • Steady-State Value $f(\infty)$: The steady-state value as $t \to \infty$
  • Time Constant $\tau$: Determines the speed of the transient process

General Formula:

$$f(t) = f(\infty) + [f(0^+) - f(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau}$$

Applicable Range: Full response of any first-order linear circuit

Applies to: first-order linear circuits $\tau = R_{eq}C$ or $\tau = L/R_{eq}$
Procedure Three-Element Method Solution Steps
1
Find $f(0^+)$
using switching rules and
the $t=0^+$ equivalent circuit
2
Find $f(\infty)$
open-circuit capacitor, short-circuit inductor
solve for DC steady state
3
Find $\tau$
set independent sources to zero,
find $R_{eq}$
4
Substitute into formula
$f(t) = f(\infty) + [f(0^+) - f(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau}$

7.5 Step Function and Step Response

7.5.1 Step Function
$$\varepsilon(t) = \begin{cases} 0, & t < 0 \\ 1, & t > 0 \end{cases}$$
  • Delayed Step: $\varepsilon(t - t_0)$ transitions at $t = t_0$
  • Amplitude Step: $K\varepsilon(t)$ with amplitude K
  • Physical Meaning: Represents a switch closing (or opening) at $t=0$
7.5.2 Step Response

Definition: The zero-state response of a first-order circuit under a step input

RC Circuit Step Response
$u_C(t) = U_s(1-e^{-t/\tau})\varepsilon(t)$
RL Circuit Step Response
$i_L(t) = \frac{U_s}{R}(1-e^{-t/\tau})\varepsilon(t)$

Significance: The step response is the basis for analyzing more complex inputs

7.6 Impulse Function and Impulse Response

7.6.1 Impulse Function
$$\delta(t) = \begin{cases} 0, & t \neq 0 \\ \infty, & t = 0 \end{cases}, \quad \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\delta(t)dt = 1$$
  • Sifting Property: $\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(t)\delta(t)dt = f(0)$
  • Physical Meaning: Represents an extremely large force acting over an extremely short time (e.g., hammer blow, lightning strike)
  • Relationship with Step Function: $\delta(t) = d\varepsilon(t)/dt$
7.6.2 Impulse Response

Definition: The zero-state response of a circuit under a unit impulse function $\delta(t)$

Solution Methods:

  • Method 1: Direct solution of the differential equation
  • Method 2: Differentiate the step response (for first-order circuits)
$$h(t) = \frac{1}{RC}e^{-t/RC}\varepsilon(t)$$

RC Circuit Impulse Response

Impulse response = derivative of step response Fourier transform pair with frequency response

7.7 Second-Order Circuit Zero-Input Response

Overview Second-Order Circuit Basic Equation

Circuit Structure: Contains two energy-storage elements (e.g., RLC series or parallel circuit)

Differential Equation: Second-order constant-coefficient linear differential equation (RLC series as example)

$$LC\frac{d^2u_C}{dt^2} + RC\frac{du_C}{dt} + u_C = 0$$

Characteristic Equation:

$$s^2 + \frac{R}{L}s + \frac{1}{LC} = 0$$

Characteristic Roots:

$$s_{1,2} = -\alpha \pm \sqrt{\alpha^2 - \omega_0^2}$$
  • $\alpha = R/(2L)$: Damping coefficient
  • $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}$: Natural angular frequency
